When Emotions Become Physical

A measured question, not a simple answer

When Emotions Become Physical

What does a self-reported symptom scale have to do with a blood marker? This comparison offers a careful starting point.

The share above 3 mg/L

Each bar is the percentage of participants in that PHQ-9 symptom group whose hs-CRP was above 3 mg/L. Denominators are shown below the group labels; values describe this complete-case sample, not the U.S. population.

The question here is whether the proportion above a commonly used hs-CRP threshold differs across PHQ-9 symptom groups in adults who completed all the selected NHANES measures. The PHQ-9 asks about nine symptoms over the previous two weeks; it is a screening measure, not a diagnosis or a complete account of someone’s emotional life.

In this sample, the observed proportions range from 33.6% to 50.0% across groups. The group denominators shown on the chart matter: the more severe symptom categories can have fewer participants, so their percentages may be less stable. These figures are unweighted descriptions, not adjusted estimates for all U.S. adults.

A 2026 NHANES analysis by Arellano and colleagues examined hs-CRP and depression symptom severity using multivariable methods. That peer-reviewed work provides context, but this project uses a complete-case subset and does not apply survey weights or reproduce its adjusted analyses. A separate 2024 cross-sectional study also examines hs-CRP alongside depressive symptoms.

NHANES is observational and these measurements do not establish which came first, explain an association, or rule out other factors. hs-CRP is a nonspecific marker of inflammation—not a measure of immune strength, emotional experience as a whole, or how quickly a person recovers.
